INTRODUCTION
The purpose of this Handbook, which constitutes the House Rules and other essential
information, is to provide guidelines that will promote harmonious occupancy of the units,
to protect Residents from annoyance and to preserve the reputation and prestige of the
Condominium thereby providing maximum enjoyment of the premises and its facilities.
The Management has the authority and responsibilities of amending the foregoing rules as
may be deemed necessary, in order to upkeep the premises in good condition and to
provide comfort and security to all its occupants.
All occupants and invitees in the Condominium shall be bound by these rules. It is the
Management's desire to create awareness among all occupants that to achieve the
common goal of condominium living, the cooperation of all occupants in complying with all
the rules and regulations is required.
We appreciate your cooperation and welcome you to 76 Shenton.

SECTION 1 - DEFINITIONS
These Rules may be referred to as the HOUSE RULES of 76 Shenton, and are to be read
in conjunction with the By-Laws laid out in the Second Schedule of The Supplement of
Building Maintenance and Strata Management Act 2004.
In these Rules, unless the context otherwise requires:
“Common Property” means All Common Property in 76 Shenton shall have the same
meaning as the term "Common Property" as defined in Section 2 of the Building
Maintenance and Strata Management Act 2004.
“Condominium” means the Condominium known as “76 Shenton” including the Common
Property.
“Guest” means a person other than the Resident and is in the Condominium at the
invitation of the Resident but does not include their maids and/or employees.
“Management” means (i) the Developer and/or the Managing Agents (as the case may be)
in relation to the period prior to the constitution of the Management Corporation for the
Condominium (ii) the Management Corporation of the Condominium in relation to the
period on or after the constitution of the Strata Title Plan in respect of the Condominium.
“Managing Agent “means a person or a company who is appointed by the Owner
Developer or Management Corporation to manage the day-to-day operation of the
Condominium.
“Owner Developer” means M/S Hong Leong House Pte Ltd.
“Relevant Authorities” shall include but not limited to Building and Construction
Authority, Energy Market Authority, National Environment Agency, Fire Safety and Shelter
Department, Singapore Telecommunications, etc.
“Resident” means The Occupier of a Lot whose definition shall, where appropriate,
include a Subsidiary Proprietor or any person authorised to occupy the Lot as a tenant or
lessee thereof and shall include the members of the family of such Occupier provided that
the term, ‘member of the family’ shall not include Guests, servants or agents.
“Subsidiary Proprietor” means a person or persons holding legal titles to a strata Lot in
76 Shenton or where separate titles are not issued yet, it includes purchasers whose name
appear in the Sale and Purchase Agreement.
“Lot” or “Unit” shall mean a stratum, which is shown as a lot on strata title plan, and
includes a lot specified as an accessory on any such plan.

C. WASTE DISPOSAL
To avoid blockage of refuse chambers caused by improper use, kindly adhere to the
following instructions: 1.

Loose or wet kitchen waste should be sealed in plastic bags before disposing into the
refuse chute. Use only suitable size (maximum size appropriately 300mm) refuse bags.
The refuse bags should easily be able to pass through the volume-controlled hopper.

2.

To avoid chokage, the following should not be thrown into the refuse chute: i.

Heavy items, appliances and / or bulky items;;

ii. Highly expandable items such as pillows and foam, crushed plastic wraps, crushed
or folded carton boxes and unwanted clothing as they will expand inside the refuse
system and cause blockage;
iii. Large quantities of books, magazines or newspapers;
iv. Long objects and bundle of ropes, blankets, etc.;
v. Flammable or burning hot items (e.g. lighted cigarette butts, hot coal) as they are
fire hazards;
vi. Construction debris such as timber, bricks, tiles, cement, concrete, sand, stones,
metals, plastics, renovation waste, etc.;
vii. Potteries from garden waste etc.;
viii.Corrosive chemicals such as acids, alkalis, paints, etc.
3.

Residents are to arrange for unwanted furniture or bulky items to be disposed out of
the Condominium at their own cost.

4.

Breakable items such as glass bottles, etc., should be properly tied up and brought
down to dispose into the appropriate recycle bin provided.

Daily Cleaning :


The materials of the Tradizionale and Flair series are made primarily of Calcium
Carbonate. For this reason, we always advise the most delicate cleaning
methods. The color and brightness of the material may be affected by acid or

everyday cleaning, it is sufficient to pass a clean humid cloth on the surface and
dry it off with a dry cloth. Alternatively, it is possible to wash the surface with a
modest amount of neutral soap, such as common Marseilles soap, and then
rinse and dry with a soft, dry cloth.

Attention :


We advise you not to use detergents, cleaning or stain removal techniques that
are not suitable for the purpose: wrong type of cleaning procedure could cause
damage that only specialized technicians could correct. We advise you to always
examine the label of the product you want to use, to ensure it is suitable for the
purpose and, in any case, we suggest you test it, according to the instructions
for use on the package, in a small area of the surface that is hidden from view.

Important :


We advise you not to leave food on the surface, such as oil (whether olive oil or
seed oil), wine, tea, coffee, fruit juices, beverages (such as Coca Cola) fruit and
vegetables (cut) or products for personal hygiene, such as toothpaste, perfumes,
after shave, etc. Some of the substances contained in these products may be
aggressive. Therefore, in order to safeguard the surface, they should be removed
as quickly as possible and the surface should be rinsed with water and dried. In
the case of oily substances, the surface should be washed with a neutral soap.





the solid part with a knife. A plastic knife would be best, but care should be

movement. Following this treatment, the surface must obviously be rinsed with
hot water and dried.

(such as Magic Rubber by Mastro Linda, Easy Erasing Pad by Scotch Brite, etc),
which can be found easily in large scale distribution centres or in shops
specialized in home cleaning products. In any case, we always advise caution in
using the above products.

have been accidentally spilled on the surface (products for the cleaning of

or paper, followed by abundant rinsing.

Timber Floor
General Precautions:







Fit protective pads to furniture legs to reduce risk of damaging the floor. To
protect the floor surface use protective mats for furniture with castors. Lay rugs
over the clean floor so dirt is not trapped underneath.
Dirt and grit can lead to excess wear and scratching of a hardwood timber floor.
Use dirt trapping mats at all exterior doors to minimize dirt, sand and grit that
might be brought inside. Small rugs and hall runners are also helpful for
collecting the dirt that can scratch a hardwood timber floor.
All hardwood timber floors will fade, darken or change shades over time.
Exposure to sunlight will increase this process. Rotate rugs periodically and
protect the floor from direct sunlight with curtains or blinds.
High heel shoes can dent a hard floor surface. Special care should be taken to
remove shoes that have exposed heels with sharp points to avoid potential
damage to a hardwood timber floor.
Pets with long nails or claws can scratch and dull the floor finish, make sure
pets nails or claws are trimmed regularly and kept blunt.

Cleaning Tips:








7.

To remove surface dirt and grit, regularly sweep or vacuum with a soft bristle
head. Keep door mats clean.
For stubborn dirt, damp-mop the floor using a well rung mop as over wetting a
floor when mopping or cleaning can change the moisture balance and cause a
floor to expand, with some resulting in cupping. For this reason, it is important
to ensure that mops and cloths are well rung so that as little water solutions as
possible wets the floor.
Never clean a floor with common household detergents, polishes, steel wool
pads, wax or similar products. These products can be too abrasive and scratch
the surface, or may make a floor dangerously slippery. Some products may leave
a film of silicon or wax on the timber which may hamper recoating of your floor
in the future. Never use Spirits of Kerosene, as any type of petroleum distillate
will degrade the coating and irreversibly dull the floor.
Wipe up spills and leaks promptly with a dry cloth or dry paper towel. For sticky
substances, moisten the cloth slightly.
Steam mops are not recommended for cleaning timber floors. Steam (moisture)
is forced into the joints of the timber and any small incisions, breaks or cracks
on the timber floor surface resulting in unusual reactions in the timber such as
cupping. The heat from the steam mop could also cause damaged to the coating
on the floor.
